01  Cosmic censorshipThe decency hypothesis

The horizon
is a courtesy.

In an ordinary collapse the horizon forms first. The singularity arrives afterwards, already wrapped, and no signal from it can reach anyone outside. Whatever the equations do at infinite density, the universe is spared the sight. Penrose named this hope cosmic censorship: every singularity comes dressed.

It is a conjecture, not a theorem. Nobody has proved that nature obeys it. Spin a black hole too fast, or load it with too much charge, and the formula for the horizon radius asks for the square root of a negative number. There is no horizon to form. The thing at the centre is simply there, in plain view, like an open wound in the sky.

Physics does not break at a naked singularity. Physics continues. That is the problem: it continues from a point where it cannot tell you what happens next.

Redaction holding. Redaction holding. Redaction leaking.
02  Observing the exposureThere is no safe distance

Nothing stalls.
Nothing fades.

At a black hole you watch things slow, redden and freeze at the edge; the horizon hides the ending. Here there is no edge. Light leaves the centre and arrives. Objects falling in do not freeze; they reach the point of infinite curvature while you watch, and the light of that arrival comes back out to you, not redshifted to nothing but blueshifted, piled up, every moment of the collapse delivered at once.

Where a photon ring should be, light tries to orbit and finds no stable path. The ring forms, tightens, and collapses into the point, then there is only the point: a region smaller than anything, brighter than its surroundings, with no size you could measure and no edge you could point to. Telescopes do not resolve it. They report it.

Look away. You already can't. The light is in your eye before you decide.

The photon ring attempting to form, and failing.
What remains is the point.
03  Beyond the Cauchy horizonThe end of prediction

The equations
stop answering.

Relativity is deterministic. Give it the universe at one instant and it hands you every instant after. That promise holds up to a surface called the Cauchy horizon. Past it, the future is no longer fixed by the past. A naked singularity puts that surface in the open, so the region where the future is unwritten includes here.

Anything consistent with the equations may emerge from the point. Not probably. Not eventually. Simply: may. Light. Matter. Field configurations that have never existed. The mathematics does not forbid it, because the mathematics has nothing to say. You are standing in a part of spacetime that the laws have declined to describe.

The horror of a black hole is that you cannot know what is inside. The horror of a naked singularity is that you can, and it does not know either.
